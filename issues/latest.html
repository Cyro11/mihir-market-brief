Three Moves That Matter

First and second order
1 / Curve

The long end is still the underwriting constraint.

What happened: Treasury's May 21 curve printed 10Y at 4.57% and 30Y at 5.10%, leaving the back end near stress levels even as stocks rallied.

Banking read: DCF-heavy sectors, sponsor acquisition models, REIT NAVs, housing affordability, utilities, and leveraged borrowers remain rate-sensitive.

2 / Oil and geopolitics

Oil relief is helpful, not settled.

Reuters reported oil falling and stocks rising Thursday on optimism around possible progress in Iran talks, after a roughly 6% oil slide Wednesday. WTI was still near $97 Friday morning on delayed public quotes.

Second order: lower oil helps transports and consumers, but a reversal would reprice inflation, rates, airlines, industrials, and energy cash flows quickly.

3 / AI financing

The IPO window is open for the right AI story.

Nvidia's results kept AI capex in the macro conversation, while Cerebras showed public investors will still fund large, thematic growth issuers.

Second order: ECM desks get a proof point, but post-debut volatility means bankers still need valuation discipline and real use-of-proceeds clarity.

IB Learning Corner

How To Read An IPO Pop

Daily skill builder

A big first-day IPO gain is not automatically "good" or "bad." It can mean the company left money on the table, or it can mean banks priced the deal to clear a large book, create liquidity, and reset the valuation conversation for a new public issuer.

For Cerebras, the banker question is whether the IPO proves durable AI demand or just scarce supply of public AI pure plays. Watch follow-on demand, lockup pressure, customer disclosures, gross margin trajectory, and whether other issuers can price without a novelty premium.
