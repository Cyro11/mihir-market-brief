What Moved Overnight

Three Moves That Matter

First and second order
1 / Inflation

PCE is not letting the Fed fade the war-price shock yet.

What happened: BEA said April PCE rose 0.4% month over month and 3.8% year over year; core PCE rose 0.2% and 3.3%, respectively.

Banking read: the monthly core print is manageable, but the headline year-over-year number and energy sensitivity still matter for debt pricing and rate-cut assumptions.

2 / Equity tone

Risk appetite is broadening beyond the chip tape.

CNBC reported stocks rose Friday, crude slipped, and all three major indexes touched fresh intraday highs, with Dell, Micron, Qualcomm, and XLK extending the AI infrastructure trade.

Second order: broader AI hardware demand can support ECM and convertibles, but it also raises input-cost and supply-chain inflation risks.

3 / M&A governance

Activism is attaching to strategic-deal risk.

Reuters reported Toms Capital built a significant McCormick stake after the Unilever Foods deal was announced; the stake size and intended push were not clear.

Second order: activist pressure can force faster synergy proof, tighter closing communication, and a clearer answer on whether the Reverse Morris Trust complexity is worth the scale.

How To Read Activism Around A Pending Deal

Daily skill builder

Activism around a pending transaction is not automatically a hostile campaign. It can be a public fight, a private engagement, a push for faster closing, a demand for divestitures, or simply leverage to improve disclosure and execution commitments.

For McCormick, the core banker question is whether the Unilever Foods combination creates enough scale and synergy value to offset dilution, regulatory timing, integration risk, and shareholder fatigue before the expected mid-2027 close.
